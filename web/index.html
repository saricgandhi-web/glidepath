How it works

  1. Tell it your situation

    One sentence: your age, savings, income, how much you save, when you want to retire and what you'll spend. Every field starts with a sensible default.

  2. It plays out 5,000 lifetimes of markets

    Each year, stock and bond returns are drawn from J.P. Morgan's and Vanguard's long-term forecasts. A lifetime succeeds if every year of retirement gets paid for.

  3. You get the changes that matter

    Solvers find the exact savings rate, spending level or retirement age that reaches 85% confidence, and stress tests show what could knock you off course.

      Questions people ask

      Is Glidepath really free?

      Yes. There's no account, no ads and nothing to upgrade to. The code is open source under the MIT license, so anyone can read it, check it or reuse it.

      Does it store or send what I type?

      No. Every calculation runs in your browser. The page is built so it can't make network requests at all, and nothing is saved: close the tab and your numbers are gone.

      What does "85% confidence" mean?

      In 85% of the simulated markets, your savings pay for every year of planned spending. It's a planning target, not a guarantee. A higher target costs more today; a lower one leaves less room for bad luck.

      How accurate is it?

      It's a model, so it's only as good as its assumptions. The market forecasts come from J.P. Morgan and Vanguard, Social Security follows the SSA's formula, and the engine is checked by 53 automated tests. Taxes, pensions and home equity aren't modeled yet.

      Is this financial advice?

      No. Glidepath is an educational tool. It can show you how the numbers move, but it doesn't know your whole situation. For decisions about taxes, insurance or investments, talk to a qualified professional.

          Returns

          Each simulated year draws a stock return and a bond return from correlated lognormal distributions. They're calibrated so the median equals the forecast compound return and the spread equals the forecast volatility. The base case uses J.P. Morgan's 2026 Long-Term Capital Market Assumptions: U.S. stocks and bonds a year, with inflation at . Vanguard's more cautious outlook is one click away.

          Your mix of stocks and bonds glides from the share you hold today to the share you want at retirement. Fees come out of every year's return, and everything is converted to today's dollars, so a number on screen means what it buys now.

          Cash flows

          Your income grows a little faster than inflation until you retire, and you contribute your savings rate each year, including any employer match. In retirement, the portfolio pays whatever your spending goal needs beyond Social Security. A simulated lifetime succeeds if every one of those withdrawals is paid in full through your plan-to age.

          Spending rules

          Most retirees adjust when markets fall. The planner can model three ways of drawing down the same portfolio:

          • Fixed: the same real amount every year. The whole risk is running out.
          • Guardrails: start at your goal; if your withdrawal rate drifts more than 20% above where it started, cut spending 10%, and if it drifts 20% below, raise it 10%. A simplified version of Guyton and Klinger (2006).
          • Percent of portfolio: withdrawals rise and fall with your balance. The money can't run out, but income swings with the market.

          Because flexible rules trade running out for spending cuts, the planner also reports typical spending, the leanest year in a bad market and the chance of a year below 80% of your goal.

          Social Security

          Your benefit is estimated with the SSA's formula: average your highest 35 years of earnings, apply the 90%, 32% and 15% bend points, then adjust for the age you claim. Claiming at 62 cuts the benefit by up to 30%; waiting past 67 adds 8% a year until 70. Your statement at ssa.gov is more precise, and you can type it in.

          An optional stress test cuts benefits to 83% from 2034, the share the 2026 Trustees Report projects will be payable once the trust funds run out.

          Solvers and stress tests

          The three levers are solved by bisection on one fixed set of simulated markets, so the answer is exact: apply the suggested savings rate or spending level and you land on 85%. The earliest retirement age is found by stepping one year at a time.

          The stress test reruns your plan under seven scenarios with the same random draws, so each change in your odds comes from the scenario alone: a crash in your first two retirement years, weaker markets, a Social Security cut, living to 100, losing your job three years early, 1% more in fees, and the three market risks together.

          Verification

          The planning engine is written twice: in Python for analysis and in JavaScript for this page. 41 Python unit tests check it against closed-form results (an annuity formula, a portfolio that should end at exactly zero, the SSA bend points). 12 parity tests then feed both engines the same market paths and require the JavaScript version to reproduce every balance to floating-point precision. Every change runs all of them on GitHub before the site deploys.

          Limits

          Not modeled yet: taxes and account types, pensions and annuities, home equity, health-care shocks and couples. Returns are independent from one year to the next, so the model doesn't capture momentum or long bear markets, and real crashes can be worse than the lognormal tails suggest. Treat the result as a well-informed estimate, not a promise.

          The math behind a good plan should be free.

          Glidepath is an open-source project that puts the core of a financial plan, a probability-based answer with clear levers, in anyone's browser.

          Advice is priced as a share of what you already have, so the people with the least to invest get the least help. Yet the heart of a retirement plan is a calculation, and a calculation can be shared.

          Private by design

          • Runs in your browser. There's no server doing the math; your device does.
          • Can't phone home. The page's security policy blocks every network request, and its fonts and code are built into this one file.
          • Remembers nothing. No accounts, cookies or storage. Close the tab and your numbers are gone.

          How it's built

          • Planning engine in Python and NumPy, with 41 unit tests against closed-form results.
          • Browser engine in plain JavaScript, proven by 12 parity tests to match the Python engine.
          • Assumptions from J.P. Morgan, Vanguard, the IRS, the SSA and Morningstar, kept in one sourced file.
          • Interface animated with GSAP, ScrollTrigger and SplitText, smooth-scrolled by Lenis, with components adapted from React Bits.
          • Delivery through GitHub Actions: every change is linted and tested, then deployed to GitHub Pages.
