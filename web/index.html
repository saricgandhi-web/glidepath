      Method and sources

      How the simulation works

      Every year, stock and bond returns are drawn from correlated lognormal distributions calibrated to institutional capital market assumptions. The portfolio shifts from stocks toward bonds as you approach retirement, fees come out of each year's return, and everything is converted to today's dollars.

      While you work, you contribute your savings rate. In retirement, the portfolio pays whatever spending Social Security doesn't cover, under the spending rule you pick. A scenario succeeds if every planned withdrawal is paid in full through your plan-to age.

      The three levers are solved by bisection on the same set of simulated markets, so applying one lands exactly on the target.

      Limits

      Taxes, pensions, home equity and one-off goals are not modeled. Social Security is estimated from your income with the SSA formula; your own statement at ssa.gov is more accurate. This is an educational tool, not investment advice.

      The problem

      Only about a third of Americans work with a financial advisor, and the typical one charges about 1% of the money they manage, every year. The math behind a good plan should be free.

      What the case study found

      Four findings from three example clients, 10,000 simulations each. Every number regenerates from one script in the repository.

        How it's built

        • Planning engine in Python and NumPy, with 41 unit tests against closed-form results.
        • Browser engine in plain JavaScript. Parity tests prove it reproduces the Python engine to floating-point precision.
        • Assumptions from J.P. Morgan and Vanguard 2026 capital market forecasts, the IRS, the SSA and Morningstar, all in one sourced file.
        • Interface animated with GSAP and ScrollTrigger, smooth scrolling by Lenis, and components adapted from React Bits.
        • Delivery through GitHub Actions: tests on every push, then automatic deployment to GitHub Pages.

        Privacy

        Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere or stored, and the page makes no network requests at all: its fonts and libraries are built into this one file.
